1. Executive Overview & Problem Statement

In India, over 200 million rural and tribal artisans depend on traditional handicrafts (handlooms, pottery, wood carving, brass metal casting, folk painting) for their daily livelihood. However, due to lack of digital literacy, linguistic barriers, and complex marketplace onboarding, these artisans lose 40% to 70% of their retail margins to exploitative regional middlemen and traders.

Dhaaga solves this structural crisis by replacing traditional high-friction e-commerce entry barriers with an Audio-First Multimodal AI Cataloging Engine. An artisan simply speaks in their mother tongue while pointing their phone camera at a handwoven saree or brass sculpture. In under 3 seconds, Dhaaga transcribes their voice, verifies their craft attributes, generates bilingual product stories (English & Hindi), optimizes the photograph into a studio-grade white backdrop, calculates fair-margin dynamic pricing, and publishes directly to a live Cloud Firestore marketplace.

5. Deep Technical Defense & Hard Engineering Questions

This section addresses the foundational architectural trade-offs, defensive justifications, and hard engineering questions regarding Dhaaga's technical stack:

Q1: Why are we NOT training or self-hosting our own Image Generation / Diffusion model?

This is one of the most critical architectural decisions made by the core engineering team. There are four decisive engineering, economic, and operational reasons:

  • 1. The "Hallucination vs. Authenticity" Trap (Crucial for Physical Crafts):
    Traditional text-to-image or image-to-image diffusion models (e.g., Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, Flux) are generative hallucination engines. If an artisan photographs an authentic handwoven Sambalpuri Ikat with specific weave count and tie-dye motifs, a generative diffusion model synthesizes a fictional pattern from random latent noise rather than preserving the artisan's exact physical weave. Selling an AI-synthesized fiction results in immediate consumer returns and fraud claims. Dhaaga requires Subject-Preserving Enhancement (Segmentation + Edge-Preserving Inpainting), NOT generative creation from scratch. Cloud vision pipelines (MagicHour / Gemini Vision + ImageSegmentationHelper.kt) preserve 100% of the artisan's raw craft pixels while only replacing the workshop background with studio lighting and pedestal shadows.
  • 2. Infrastructure & Compute Economics:
    Hosting a dedicated GPU cluster (e.g., NVIDIA A100/H100 or L40S) on AWS/GCP to run custom diffusion inference costs $1,800 to $3,200 per instance per month. In rural artisan clusters, cataloging activity is heavily bursty (peaks during daylight hours 10 AM – 4 PM, nearly zero at night). A self-hosted cluster burns budget during idle hours. Serverless multimodal API invocations (Gemini / MagicHour) charge purely per-invocation (~$0.015 per catalog entry) with zero idle infrastructure cost.
  • 3. Edge Hardware Constraints on Low-Cost Rural Android Phones:
    Over 92% of Indian artisans operate sub-₹12,000 budget Android smartphones (MediaTek Helio G-series, Unisoc T606, Snapdragon 680) with 3GB–4GB RAM. Running an on-device diffusion model (even 4-bit quantized SD-Turbo via ONNX/NCNN) requires a minimum of 6GB–8GB of unified RAM and a powerful NPU. On typical artisan devices, running local diffusion triggers instant OutOfMemory (OOM) kernel kills, thermal throttling, and 30–40% battery drain per generation.
  • 4. Scarcity of Indian Heritage Craft Datasets:
    Fine-tuning a proprietary foundation model requires a minimum of 500,000+ curated, high-resolution annotated image pairs of authentic Indian handicrafts across all 28 states. Such a dataset currently does not exist in open-source research. Training prematurely would yield distorted, westernized outputs. Dhaaga's strategic Phase 2 plan is to use our live platform to organically compile the first verified national repository of Indian artisan craft photography, and subsequently train a lightweight ControlNet LoRA adapter.

Q2: How do we prevent urban traders & factory counterfeiters from creating fake artisan accounts?

Dhaaga enforces a 3-Tier Proof-of-Craft Verification Protocol:

  • Geofenced Cluster Verification: Artisan registration captures device GPS coordinates. The registration is validated against recognized rural artisan clusters (e.g., Channapatna for toys, Madhubani district for Mithila paintings, Varanasi for brocades). Accounts attempting registration from urban industrial zones are flagged for manual review.
  • Live Video Proof-of-Work (PoW): Artisans record a brief 5-second video showing their active handloom, wood-carving tools, or pottery wheel. Gemini Vision analyzes the video frames to detect authentic hand tools and physical work-in-progress.
  • Weaver Cooperative / Guild Cryptographic Vouching: Recognized artisan Self-Help Groups (SHGs) and weaver societies have guild master accounts that digitally verify individual member artisans.

Q3: How does the Voice Auto-Cataloger handle heavy rural accents and ambient workshop noise?

Artisan workshops are loud environments filled with rhythmic handloom clatter, hammer strikes, and rural ambient chatter. Dhaaga implements a multi-stage noise suppression pipeline:

  • Acoustic Pre-Filtering: Uses Android's built-in NoiseSuppressor and AcousticEchoCanceler hardware audio effect APIs on the recording audio stream to filter repetitive mechanical noise frequencies.
  • Colloquial Craft Phoneme Mapping: Rural artisans use localized terminology (e.g. zari, kadwa, tanchoi, ikat, dhokra, kalamkari) that off-the-shelf speech engines misinterpret. Dhaaga passes raw phonetic text to Gemini 2.5 Flash with custom prompt context injected with regional craft glossaries, mapping vernacular descriptions into standardized catalog fields.

Q4: How does the app prevent race-condition overselling of one-of-a-kind handmade items?

Handmade crafts are inherently unique — often an artisan has only 1 piece of an intricate tapestry. If two buyers tap "Checkout" at the exact same millisecond:

  • Dhaaga utilizes Cloud Firestore Atomic Transactions (runTransaction). During checkout, the transaction locks the product document, reads the latest stockQuantity, and only decrements and confirms if stockQuantity >= requestedQuantity.
  • If another transaction completes first and stock hits 0, the second transaction safely rolls back and notifies the buyer: "This unique handcrafted craft was just claimed by another buyer."

6. Geographical Indication (GI) Tag Implementation Architecture

A Geographical Indication (GI) is an intellectual property certification granted under the Government of India's Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999. It certifies that a craft possesses qualities, reputation, or characteristics that are essentially attributable to its specific geographic origin (e.g. Banarasi Brocade, Kashmir Pashmina, Channapatna Toys, Madhubani Art).

Official GI Tag End-to-End Technical Workflow in Dhaaga

Government Registry Cross-Referencing & Buyer Verification
GI Act, 1999 Compliant

Step 1: Master GI Database in Firestore (gi_registry)

A dedicated collection storing official GI numbers, registered craft names, authorized districts (GeoJSON spatial boundaries), and the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (CGPDTM) inspection body details.

Step 2: Artisan Authorized User KYC

During seller onboarding in ProfileSetupScreen.kt, the artisan inputs their government-issued Authorized User Number (format: AU/XXXX/GI/XXX) or uploads their physical GI Artisan Identity Card.

Step 3: Geofenced Cluster Validation

When an artisan lists a GI-tagged craft, Dhaaga verifies that the device GPS coordinates fall within the statutory geographic polygon of that craft (e.g. Banarasi Silk must originate from Varanasi/Chandauli/Mirzapur/Jaunpur).

Step 4: Buyer Trust Badging & Provenance Sheet

Verified products display an official gold-embossed Government of India GI Emblem on product cards. Tapping opens an interactive Provenance Sheet showing the weaver's authorized registration, craft heritage history, and certificate link.

Step 5: Cryptographic Tamper-Proof QR Label on Physical Packaging

When an order is confirmed, Dhaaga's backend generates a cryptographically signed QR code printed directly on the Delhivery courier shipping label (e.g., https://dhaaga.in/verify/GI-132-AU8921). When the package arrives at the buyer's home, scanning the seal displays the artisan's photograph, workshop GPS coordinates, and Government GI certification, providing 100% counterfeit protection.
